# View Framing

How a camera keeps showing what it was meant to show when the viewport's size changes, when it is shown in another viewport (a named view in the editor and in an embed), and when the model's geometry changes (edits, configurations). Implementation: [`framing.ts`](../src/cad/src/components/framing.ts) (fitting, margins, `meet`), the camera in [`Viewer.tsx`](../src/cad/src/components/Viewer.tsx), [`CameraState`](../src/cad/src/types.ts), the worker's `refs` request ([`protocol.ts`](../src/cad/src/kernel/protocol.ts)); tests: [`framing.test.ts`](../src/cad/test/framing.test.ts), [`cad-framing.spec.ts`](../src/harness/e2e/tests/cad-framing.spec.ts).

## The problem

A camera stored as direction, target, distance and orthographic zoom shows more or less of the model depending on the viewport, for three reasons:

1. **three.js fixes the vertical field of view.** A perspective camera's `aspect` only widens or narrows what it shows across, so the visible height at the target is constant in model units, and the visible width follows the viewport's aspect. Making the viewport taller, as between the screenshots of a 1.8:1 and a 0.9:1 view, halves the visible width: the model is cropped left and right and shown larger.
2. **A bounding sphere ignores the viewport.** Framing at three times the radius of the sphere around the model's bounding box gives the sphere an angular radius of asin(1/3) = 19.5°, which fits in the 22.5° half-height of a 45° camera, but horizontally only for an aspect of at least tan 19.5° / tan 22.5° = 0.85. And a sphere around a box is loose: a long flat part leaves a wide margin above and below.
3. **"Still fits" can only be guessed.** Whether a build should reframe the model, e.g. after a configuration switch, depends on what the camera shows, which a distance doesn't say without the viewport.

## Prior art

- **SVG** separates the region to show from the viewport: a `viewBox` and `preserveAspectRatio`. `meet` scales so that "The entire viewBox is visible within the viewport" and "is scaled up as much as possible", `slice` so that "The entire viewport is covered by the viewBox" ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Attribute/preserveAspectRatio)). CSS's `object-fit: contain`/`cover` is the same pair.
- **Fusion's API** stores this region for an orthographic camera as `setExtents(width, height)` in model units, and fits it with `meet`: "When the camera is assigned to a viewport, typically only the width or the height is used depending on the aspect ratio of the viewport. For example, if the width and height are both 10 but the viewport is 2:1 … the height extent will be 10 and the width extent will be recomputed to be 20" ([Camera.setExtents](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-360-API/files/Camera_setExtents.htm)). For perspective, "The extents of a perspective camera is defined by a combination of the position of the eye point … and the perspective angle". A camera can also be a fit instead: with `isFitView`, "when this camera is applied to a viewport it will modify the camera such that the entire model is displayed in the viewport" (quoted in [the Autodesk forum](https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/fusion-api-and-scripts-forum/script-to-set-current-view-to-home-fit-to-view/td-p/5525046)).
- **Blender**'s camera can keep the field of view of the larger side instead of the height: Sensor Fit "Auto: Calculates a square sensor size based on the larger of the Resolution dimensions" ([manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cameras.html)). That halves the problem: going from landscape to portrait swaps which side is kept, but a 2:1 and a 1:1 viewport still show different amounts.
- **OCCT** fits a bounding box with a margin: `V3d_View::FitAll(theMargin=0.01)`, "the margin coefficient for view borders", must be in [0, 1) ([V3d_View](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_v3d___view.html)).
- **camera-controls** (three.js) fits a box "using the nearest axis", by the larger of height and width ÷ aspect, plus half the depth, with a `cover` option and paddings "in unit" ([CameraControls.ts](https://github.com/yomotsu/camera-controls/blob/main/src/CameraControls.ts)): `heightToFit * 0.5 / Math.tan( fov * 0.5 ) + depth * 0.5`. Adding half the depth is conservative, not tight.

## Margins to the silhouette

The idea to evaluate: store, per viewport edge, the distance from the model's outline to that edge, negative for a close-up of a part of the model; when shown, move the camera back while an edge is too close, and forward until one edge is exactly as close as stored.

- **It is `meet`.** Four stored margins outline a rectangle around the silhouette; "back until none is too close, forward until one touches" is the largest scale at which that rectangle is inside the viewport. With a different aspect, two margins grow, as in Fusion's example. So resizing works.
- **The unit needs care.** Pixels depend on the viewport, fractions of the viewport are circular; model units at the target, as Fusion's extents, work.
- **It anchors every view to the whole model's outline.** For a view of everything, that is the intent: a wider configuration stays fully visible. For a close-up, it is not: a configuration that only lengthens the plate to the right moves the outline, so a view of the left hole with a negative right margin slides away from the hole. A close-up is anchored to its place in the model, or to the part it shows, not to the silhouette.
- **It costs a fit per show,** which is cheap ([below](#fitting)), but needs the meshes before the camera can be placed, so an embed shows nothing until the build.

So the margins are the right model for views that fit the model, and a fixed region for close-ups. Fusion's API leaves that choice to the caller (`isFitView` or extents); the viewer makes it from what the camera shows.

## The design

**A camera stores the region it shows, not a distance.** [`CameraState`](../src/cad/src/types.ts) has `extent: [width, height]`, the visible rectangle in the plane through the target, in model units. Shown in a viewport, the camera fits the rectangle with `meet`: the visible height is `max(height, width / aspect)`; orthographic shows it directly. In perspective, the eye's distance decides how strongly the model's near parts look larger than its far ones, so it must not depend on the viewport: `eye` stores it, in multiples of the distance at which the rectangle just fits a square 45° view (1 if not given), and the field of view widens or narrows to show the rectangle instead. Moving the eye back to fit a narrower viewport, as a fixed field of view must, flattens the perspective, so that a part's far end that showed in a wide view disappears behind its near end in a tall one. A viewport keeps the camera it was given, and a resize only refits it, so shrinking and growing back returns to the same picture. Orthographic shows what perspective shows at the target from the same eye: a dolly moves the eye instead of zooming, so that `eye` and a switch of projection keep the perspective. The clipping planes enclose everything drawn wherever the eye is; orthographic's near plane may lie behind the eye, and its rays start there.

Cameras are stored in three places: the named views and the changed Home in the model document ([18](18-named-views.md#storage)), and each user's last camera per model and tool in the browser's `localStorage` ([10](10-view-state.md#camera)). Those stored with `distance` and `zoom` (`LegacyCamera`) are converted when read (`cameraOf`), as `height = heightAt(distance) / zoom`, `width = height`, which shows what they showed in a landscape viewport, from the same eye; the next save writes the new form. The types make every reader convert: documents hold a `StoredCamera`, the viewer takes one, and named views and the user's stored cameras hand out `CameraState`s.

**A camera that shows all of the model stores its margins around it.** When the user's move ends (an orbit, dolly, pan, ViewCube turn or projection switch), the viewer checks whether every vertex of the model's meshes and every point of its shown sketches is in the view. A camera that cuts off at most 2 % of the view's width or height of the model on a side was meant to show all of it, too (`SLIVER`). If so, the camera stores `fit: [top, right, bottom, left]`, the distances from the model's fitted rectangle ([below](#fitting)) to the visible one's edges, as fractions of the fitted rectangle's height or width; else it is a close-up and stores its region. Shown again, it fits the model, adds the margins, then `meet`s. The viewport's shape adds room on one axis, e.g. above and below a wide bar in a tall view, which isn't the camera's: that axis is measured as if it had the other's scale (`trimmed`), so that the margins hold in a viewport of any shape. A view has no type to choose: "+" and "Update to current view" store whichever the camera is. A given camera with only a region, e.g. one stored before research/20, is classified the same way once the model shows in it, so that an old view of the whole model frames it in another configuration, too. The default Home is `fit: [0.1, 0.1, 0.1, 0.1]`. Because the user's own camera is stored the same way, a resize keeps its margins too, also when it isn't a named view.

A ViewCube click on a camera with margins fits the model from the new direction with the same margins. In perspective, the tightest frame of a model isn't centred on its middle (a plate seen from above and in front looks larger at its near edge, so the view's axis passes 47 units beside the middle of a 200 × 200 plate), and a turn around that axis's point would move the model off the screen. A close-up turns around its target.

"Close-up" is this document's term. Fusion's "Detail View" is something else, a drawing view: "An enlarged view of a portion of another drawing view" ([Fusion Drawing reference](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-Drawing/files/DWG-REF-DRAWING-TAB.htm)).

### Close-ups

A close-up follows what it shows. When the user's move ends on one, the viewer finds the faces and shown sketches entirely in it, and its margins to their fitted rectangle, which are never negative: `subject: { faces: RefData[]; sketches: string[]; fit: Margins }`. A face is stored as the reference a pick of it writes, as data, as a collaborator's selection is ([13](13-collaboration.md)); the worker makes them for all faces at once (a `refs` request). Sketches are stored by their feature's name. Things embedded in the model, once there are any ([00](00-status.md#not-started)), will join them. A named view stores all of them, however many. The user's own last camera doesn't keep the subject, as it is stored after every move ([10](10-view-state.md#camera)): reopened, a close-up shows its stored region. Shown again, the viewer resolves the faces in the shown bodies (the worker's `locate`), fits them and the sketches as the whole model is fitted ([below](#fitting)) and adds the margins. A body doesn't do: a close-up of a hole shows a part of the plate, not the plate.

The references hold the faces' topological names, the ones that code references resolve ([03](03-topological-naming.md#recommendation)): rooted in the feature that made the face, e.g. `extrude1.side.front`, and extended by splits, so a name that splits resolves to all its pieces, which the fit then contains. They survive configurations as references in the code do, and break where those break ([17](17-parameters.md#parameters-stress-references)). Faces that no longer resolve, e.g. of a feature that a configuration's loop parameter removed, are left out; when none resolves, the close-up shows its stored region. When the named faces move apart, the close-up widens to keep them all.

A camera with nothing entirely inside it, e.g. one inside a single large face, shows nothing to look at, so it is taken as not meant that way: it stores the face that covers most of its view (sampled on a 12 × 12 grid of rays), and is shown fitting that face with the default margin. A camera that shows no part of the model at all stores the default Home's margins from its direction.

The subject is found after the move, in the worker, so a view made within milliseconds of a move is stored as its region.

### Fitting

For a direction, express the corners of the bounding box of each body, or of each face, and the sketches' points, in camera coordinates (x right, y up, z towards the eye), with `t` the tangent of half the horizontal or vertical field of view. Per axis, with `a = max(x + t·z)` and `b = max(−x + t·z)`, the eye must be at least at depth `(a + b) / (2t)`, centred at `x = (a − b) / 2`. The fitted rectangle lies in the plane through the points' middle depth, as wide and high as each axis's narrowest frustum is there. Fits are measured with a square 45° lens whatever the viewport (`fitLens`), so that margins and `eye` don't depend on the viewport's shape. Shown in a viewport, the eye stays at its distance and the field of view is the larger of what `meet` needs for the rectangle and what every point needs (`tanToShow`), as the near and far parts of the model look larger or smaller than the plane through its middle. That is the tightest perspective fit of these points for the direction ([`framing.test.ts`](../src/cad/test/framing.test.ts): for random point sets and aspects, every point is inside the frustum and one is on it). Orthographic is the projected bounding rectangle. The box corners make it slightly loose for bodies rotated against the axes; mesh vertices would be tight, but cost a pass over every vertex per fit, and a camera with margins fits again on every frame of a resize drag. Whether the model is in the view, which decides between margins and a close-up, is checked on the vertices, once per move; the box corners of a slanted face may stick out of a view that shows all of it, so margins are at least zero.

In perspective, margins are fractions in the plane through the model's middle, so on the screen the model's nearest and farthest parts span a little more or less: the default Home showed a 200 × 40 × 10 bar and a 200 × 200 plate across 0.81 to 0.82 of the view in the e2e tests, not exactly 1 / 1.2.

### When the geometry changes

A camera given to the view, i.e. a named view, Home, an embed's view, or the camera a tool opens with, applies its margins or its subject to the new geometry exactly after every build, until the user moves it: a configuration that halves the model shows it as large as before.

The user's own camera follows the geometry less eagerly. It refits with its margins, keeping its direction, when the model leaves the view, or when the model with the camera's margins would fill less than half of it (`REFIT_BELOW` in [`Viewer.tsx`](../src/cad/src/components/Viewer.tsx)), which felt right in use. Within that band, an edit doesn't move the camera, so that the user sees a parameter change make the part bigger or smaller instead of the camera undoing it, and the camera keeps the margins it had, which a refit or a resize shows again. A close-up refits, with the default margins, only when no part of the model is left in its view.

Feature dialogs cover part of the view; framing doesn't leave room for them.

**Blender's Auto sensor fit is not needed:** with extents and `meet`, the fixed axis no longer decides what is visible, and a fixed vertical field of view keeps perspective distortion the same across viewports.
